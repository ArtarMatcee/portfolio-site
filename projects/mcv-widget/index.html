UX / UI case study

MCV Widget

A set of home screen widgets for MCV, drawn against Apple’s widget templates for iPhone and iPad. Four of them — Assignment Reminder, Class Schedule, Announcements and Quick Action — put what a student needs right now on the home screen, so the answer is already there before MCV is opened.

Design overview

Personal, informational, contextual

The file opens with three words a widget has to earn before it takes space on a home screen. Everything that follows is measured against them: the widget shows this student’s own work, it answers rather than asks, and what it shows depends on the moment it is looked at.

The product
MCV Widget is a set of iOS home- and lock-screen widgets for MyCourseVille, Chulalongkorn University’s learning management system — four widgets, drawn for iPhone and iPad at every size each device supports.
The problem
Coursework lives behind a web login. A student has to decide to go and look, which means the things that matter most — what is due today, whether the class moved — are the things found last.
  • Personal

    This student’s week

    Named courses, real deadlines, the room the next class is in — not a shared feed.

  • Informational

    An answer, at a glance

    A row is a title, a course and a time. Nothing has to be tapped to be read.

  • Contextual

    True right now

    Today’s deadlines, the next class, the newest announcement — and when there is nothing left, the widget says so.

Why each widget exists

The file records three problems with reaching MCV through the web, and what the widget does about each one.

  1. Problem 01

    Reminders that will not clear

    On the web, work due inside seven days stays on the reminder list — it only goes away once submit is pressed.

    In the widget: assignments are ordered by the nearest deadline and counted, so the day is easier to plan. Hand one in and it leaves the widget — clearing something off a list is meant to feel good.

  2. Problem 02

    Announcements that go unseen

    Important notices get missed — a cancelled class, for instance.

    In the widget: announcements sit where they are read the moment the phone is picked up, without opening the web.

  3. Problem 03

    Features buried behind a login

    Reaching a feature means opening the web and signing in, and some of them sit several screens deep.

    In the widget: the destinations that get used most are on the home screen, one tap from anywhere.

The four widgets

  • Assignment Reminder

    Work still to hand in, nearest deadline first, with a ring that reads how much of the day is already cleared.

    Small · Medium · Large on both devices — plus Extra Large on iPad

  • Class Schedule

    Small · Medium · Large on both devices — plus Extra Large on iPad

  • Announcements

    The newest posts, each with its course, its subject and the day it went up.

    Small · Medium · Large on both devices — plus Extra Large on iPad

  • Quick Action

    Four destinations that get used most — Notifications, Assignments, My courses and To do list — straight from the home screen.

Main design showcase

Three widgets, close up

The three information widgets, each at Extra Large on iPad — the size with the most room and the most decisions in it. Numbered pins mark the element each note is about.

Problem
A widget cannot be scrolled, searched or asked a question. Whatever it shows has to already be the answer at the moment the phone is picked up.
Decision
Let the size decide the scope, not the content. Small and Medium are today; Large and Extra Large open out into Monday to Sunday, and each row becomes its own tap target.
Constraint
There is no room for a second label. The count of outstanding work sits inside a ring filled to the ratio of everything due against what is left, so how much is already handed in is read from the shape instead of written out.

01 — Assignment Reminder

Everything still to hand in, ordered by how soon it is due

At Small and Medium the widget is today’s list. At Large and Extra Large it opens out into the whole week, Monday to Sunday, and each row becomes its own tap target.

1 2 3 4

  1. 1 The week, not the day

    Small and Medium are headed with the date and the word Due. From Large up the heading changes to This Week / Assignments, and the content changes with it: Monday to Sunday instead of today only. Tapping a row opens that assignment; on the smaller sizes, tapping the widget opens All Assignments.

  2. 2 A gauge instead of a number

    The ring fills to the ratio of everything due against what is left.

  3. 3 Sorted by the next deadline

    Days are headed with an SF Symbols calendar glyph and an amber weekday. Inside a day, rows run earliest deadline first — so with one assignment due at 12:00 and another at 23:59, the noon one sits on top. Each row is the title, the course underneath in slate, and the deadline in amber.

  4. 4 Two columns, one week

    Extra Large is twice as wide as Large but no taller. Rather than stretch the rows, the week is split: the first day runs down the left, the next days pick up in a second column. Large fits five assignments across two days; Extra Large fits ten across three, and nothing shrank to do it.

  5. 5 Nothing due today

    Hand the last thing in and the list does not sit empty — the widget swaps to a state of its own: an icon, “Nothing due today”, and the line “Go touch some grass”. Every size has one, in both appearances.

02 — Class Schedule

Where to be next, and how long there is until then

The small sizes answer one question — what is next, when does it start and finish, which room. Extra Large answers a different one: what does the next three days actually look like.

1 2 3 4

  1. 1 Three days at a glance

    Extra Large is the only size that leaves the list behind. It is headed simply Class Schedule under a calendar.badge.clock glyph, and gives the next three days a column each.

  2. 2 An hour rail, not a list

    Each day is an hour scale with a hairline per hour, and classes are blocks placed against it. The current day is given two rails side by side so its whole day fits, which also makes it the widest column — the one the eye lands on first.

  3. 3 Length you can see

    A block is as tall as the class is long and carries a coloured bar down its left edge, so a two-hour class and a one-hour class are told apart before either label is read. Gaps in the day read as gaps.

  4. 4 Next class first

    Small, Medium and Large keep the list. They lead with Next class and its start time set large, then the block itself — 11:00–12:30, the course, the room — and Medium and Large add Up next underneath for the rest of the day. Tapping opens that course in MCV.

  5. 5 No more classes today

    The day runs out and the widget swaps to “No more classes today” with the line “Remember to pace yourself”, under a studentdesk glyph.

03 — Announcements

The newest post, then as many more as the size allows

One layout, scaled by how much room there is: the latest announcement on Small, and fourteen of them on Extra Large — with the newest always at the top.

1 2 3

  1. 1 One heading, one megaphone

    Every size carries the same heading — a megaphone.fill glyph, the small word above, and Announcements set in blue beneath it. It is the one fixed thing across seven different layouts.

  2. 2 One post, three facts

    A post is its subject in white, the course it came from in slate underneath, and the date it went up in amber on the right. The newest one is set a size larger and given the width of the heading, so it reads as the lead rather than as row one.

  3. 3 Density is the variable

    Nothing about a row changes between sizes — only how many fit. Small shows the latest one; iPad Medium three, Large six, Extra Large fourteen; iPhone Medium four and Large seven. Each row is its own tap target and opens that announcement in MCV.

  4. 4 No more announcements

    Read them all and the widget says so: “No more announcements”, with the line “Cherish your downtime”. All three information widgets end the same way — a quiet line rather than a blank card.

Three ways of saying “you are done”

An empty widget is still a widget. Each one pairs a line of fact with a line of encouragement, under the same glyph it uses when it has something to show.

Variants and states

Every size, both appearances, both states

Problem
A widget is not one design. Four widgets across two devices, four sizes, light and dark, with content and without — and any one of those combinations is the one a student actually ends up looking at.
Decision
Draw every combination rather than a representative few, and put the explorer on this page so each one can be checked at the size and appearance it will really appear in.
Constraint
Each variant is the export from the frame it was drawn in, at its true proportions — not a single design scaled up and down to fake the others.

In context

On the device it was drawn for

Problem
A widget can look right on its own and wrong on a home screen, where it holds a fixed share of the display and competes with everything around it.
Decision
Show them on the device they were drawn for, at true scale — the lock-screen composition as the design has it, and an iPad home screen at the real proportions of an 11-inch display.
Constraint
The iPad shell, the wallpaper and the MCV caption were built for this page and are not part of the design. They are labelled as such so the mockup is never read as the work.

Lock screen

Two of the widgets were also drawn for the lock screen, as rectangular widgets under the clock — white type straight onto the wallpaper, no card behind it. Assignment Reminder lists the three assignments closest to their deadline; Class Schedule gives the time, the course and the room of the class that is coming.

Lock Screen frames, with the rectangular widgets placed over them exactly as they sit in the design.

Presentation mockup

iPad home screen

Extra Large is 628 × 300 pt — a little over half the width of an 11-inch iPad in landscape, and four of the six columns its home screen grid is built from. That is the room the three-day timetable needs. The widget here is shown at its true share of the screen; the shell, the wallpaper and the MCV label under it were made for this page and are not part of the original design.
